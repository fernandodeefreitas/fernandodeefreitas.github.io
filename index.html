02 — Case studies

Applied at work

As Customer Experience Supervisor leading a small collections team at a student-accommodation operator in Dublin, I design the spreadsheets, scripts and tracking tools the team runs on every day. Details below are described at a process level — no tenant, client or company data.

01

Risk-tiered collections tracker

Problem. A growing receivables book with no consistent way to tell which overdue accounts needed attention first — collectors were working cases roughly in the order they came up, not by risk.

Approach. Built a Google Sheets tracker fed by a daily financial export, with Apps Script automating the import: enriching rows via lookups, calculating days overdue, and tagging each account into a risk tier (from "Risk C", 0–3 days, up to "Risk A15+", 15 or more days overdue). Dated snapshots let the team compare delinquency day over day instead of only seeing a single current state.

Impact. The team can now triage by risk tier instead of by gut feel, with a repeatable daily refresh that used to be a manual, error-prone copy-paste job.

  • Google Sheets
  • Apps Script
  • VLOOKUP / data enrichment
02

Team productivity platform

Problem. As the team lead, I had no single view of what each collector had actually done in a day — contacts made, value recovered, cases resolved, time-to-resolution — without asking each person individually.

Approach. Designing a lightweight web platform that ingests the team's daily collections export (downloaded as Excel from the shared tracker) and turns it into per-agent and team-level activity metrics, instead of requiring agents to log actions by hand.

Impact. Turns a scattered, manual check-in process into a shared source of truth for coaching and workload balancing, built around how the team already works rather than adding a new tool they have to adopt.

  • Web app
  • Excel ingestion
  • Productivity metrics
03

Cross-booking payment reconciliation

Problem. Payments and deposits don't always map cleanly onto a single booking — a tenant might have several linked bookings, roommates sharing a reservation, or a deposit that carries over from one booking cycle to the next. Reconciling these by eye was slow and error-prone.

Approach. Built a repeatable side-by-side reconciliation layout that lines up each booking's contracted amounts against what was actually paid, person by person, so linked bookings and carried-over deposits are checked together rather than one booking at a time.

Impact. Billing corrections that used to require piecing together several records by hand now follow a standard template, cutting down the back-and-forth needed to confirm what's actually owed.

  • Google Sheets
  • Reconciliation design
  • Billing QA
